# Analysis and judgment

## Financial definitions and quality

Show each material metric's definition, period and accounting basis. Use INR crore for monetary totals, crore shares for counts, INR per share for price/EPS and decimals for model rates. Never mix currencies; document a dated conversion where needed.

- Growth uses comparable periods. CAGR requires positive endpoints and actual elapsed years; use absolute changes for loss-to-profit transitions.
- Operating margin = EBIT/revenue. PAT margin identifies whether profit is attributable to owners. EPS/equity valuation uses common-shareholder profit and diluted common shares.
- ROE = annual attributable profit / average opening and closing common equity. ROCE = annual EBIT / average capital employed; define and reconcile the denominator across peers. Do not label ROCE as ROIC or mechanically annualize seasonal quarterly earnings.
- Nonfinancial cash conversion = CFO / comparable positive PAT; examine cumulative conversion and working capital. Negative PAT makes this ratio unsuitable.
- Sector routing can make a derived ratio inapplicable without making its disclosed source amounts unavailable. Preserve reported bank CFO as a fact while withholding industrial CFO/PAT and net-debt judgments; do not replace known amounts with null merely because a valuation method does not use them.
- CFO minus cash capex is a cash-generation diagnostic, **not automatically FCFF or owner earnings**. Disclose capex definition, acquisitions, interest classification and maintenance/growth capex uncertainty.
- Treat leases, restricted cash, debt-like liabilities and minorities consistently. Match enterprise value to operating metrics and equity value to shareholder earnings/book value. Never subtract net debt twice.
- Examine recurring earnings, tax/other-income distortions, receivable/inventory growth, capitalization, cash availability, dividend funding, debt maturities, guarantees and litigation. Discuss material audit concerns explicitly and fairly.
- Reconcile splits/bonuses and issuance with per-share values. Forecast diluted share counts and account consistently for capital-raise proceeds. Never compare post-split price with pre-split EPS.

## Sector routing

| Type | Analysis emphasis | Valuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profitable nonfinancial | Margins, reinvestment, cash conversion, leverage, competitive durability | Scenario P/E or EV/EBITDA; FCFF DCF when defensible |
| Bank/NBFC | NPA/stage-3 trends, provisioning, credit costs, ROA/ROE, capital adequacy, funding, liquidity/maturity mismatch, concentration | P/B linked to sustainable ROE, growth and cost of equity; P/E cross-check; justified equity/excess-return models |
| Insurer | Solvency, underwriting, persistency/claims; embedded/new-business value for life insurers | Sector-specific equity valuation, not industrial cash-flow rules |
| Cyclical/commodity | Mid-cycle prices/margins, capacity, survival, capex discipline | Normalized earnings/multiples; avoid mistaking peak-cycle low P/E for cheapness |
| Conglomerate/holding company | Segment economics, ownership, capital allocation, holding liabilities | Sum of parts with explicit net-debt allocation and justified discounts; avoid double counting |
| Loss-making/short history | Unit economics, funding runway, dilution, path to sustainable profit | Transparent operating scenarios; no negative-P/E or unsupported terminal profits |

Methods beyond the helper can use an explicit reproducible calculation saved with assumptions in the run. Do not force every company into supported methods. Missing credible valuation inputs can justify Insufficient evidence even when qualitative work is possible.

## Scenarios

Forecast operations before valuation. Explain revenue drivers, margins/credit costs, capital requirements, financing and dilution. Ground exit multiples in comparable economics and dated peer/history evidence; do not assume the highest historical multiple returns.

- Long-term: bear/base/bull at both 3 and 5 years. Match forecast periods to exit dates.
- One-year: bear/base/bull at the exact deadline. Label exit financial estimates as trailing, forward or run-rate. A catalyst after the deadline cannot support the base case without a specific earlier mechanism. Include delay/no-catalyst stress, funding, liquidity and downside until exit.
- Produce ranges from explicit assumptions. Use verified entry quotes; show cost-basis returns separately. Distinguish past dividends received from future dividends.
- Holding-period return = (exit price + interval cash dividends − entry price) / entry price. Annualized terminal-wealth return = ((exit price + dividends)/entry price)^(1/years) − 1, assuming dividends held as cash, not reinvested; it is not IRR. Emphasize holding-period returns for intervals under a year.
- Updates to fixed deadlines use remaining time. Do not annualize original purchase-price returns without acquisition date and intervening dividends.
- Do not assign unsupported scenario probabilities.

FCFF DCF values the business **today**: discount explicit annual FCFF at justified nominal INR WACC; terminal value = FCFF_last × (1+g)/(WACC−g), requiring WACC > g. Stable growth must be economically supportable. Reconcile enterprise value to common equity using debt, cash, minorities and other claims; divide by current diluted shares. Explain terminal-value dependence and test WACC ±1 percentage point and terminal growth ±0.5 point where valid. CFO-minus-capex needs financing/tax adjustments before use as FCFF. Banks/NBFCs use equity models.

## Action recommendation and exits

Assess business quality, valuation, risk and confidence independently with evidence. Do not invent a weighted aggregate score or universal 'good P/E/ROE' threshold.

| Action | Required reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| Buy | For a prospective purchase, quality and price offer a justified risk-adjusted margin of safety within this horizon. Specify entry conditions, intended exit window, downside and required assumptions. Label any return hurdle as user-specified or an analyst assumption. For an existing holder, recommend additional buying only if the user asks about adding; otherwise assess Hold/Sell. |
| Hold | User already owns the stock and retaining it remains supported by prospective returns and risks over the remaining horizon. State exit/review conditions. Hold is not an automatic recommendation to buy more or wait to recover the original purchase price. |
| Sell | User already owns the stock and supported thesis, governance, solvency, valuation or deadline-mismatch risks favor exiting. Specify Sell now or a justified conditional exit within the selected period; explain the tradeoff of waiting. Do not recommend holding until the deadline when the reason to sell is already present. |
| Wait | For a prospective purchase, the company merits attention but entry valuation, timing or a bounded uncertainty warrants waiting. Specify the condition needed before buying. For an existing position assess Hold/Sell instead of an ambiguous Wait. |
| Avoid buying | For a user who does not own the stock, supported business, governance, solvency, valuation or deadline-mismatch risks make buying unattractive. This is not a Sell or short-selling instruction. |
| Insufficient evidence | Missing/conflicting essential identity, filings, price or valuation inputs prevents a defensible decision. Identify dependent conclusions withheld. |

When ownership is unknown, present conditional actions such as **If not owned: Wait. If already owned: Hold**, with a reason for each. Do not assume the two actions are interchangeable: an existing holding may merit retention without meeting the hurdle for a fresh purchase. Assess either from prospective economics, not purchase-price anchoring. Do not force a Buy/Sell binary when Hold, Wait or Insufficient evidence is better supported.

If the current quote is missing, retain quality analysis and dated conditional ranges but no unconditional current-price Buy recommendation. A lack of findings does not prove clean governance. Separate disclosure coverage from subjective forecast confidence. The action and its timing must appear in the final chat, not only inside the linked report.

Keep the analysis horizon, action timing, and exit conditions distinct. Use an exact deadline when supplied or derived by one-year mode. For 3–5 years, retain that window rather than inventing a forced sale date. A planned exit within 12 months does not by itself mean Sell now; identify what supports buying/holding until an exit condition or the deadline. If the user requires a hard sale deadline, never imply it can be extended to wait for a target. Price ranges are model-based conditions, not promised executable prices. Do not automatically translate an old Attractive/Watch/Avoid rating into an ownership-specific action without reassessing the evidence.

Present strongest bull/bear cases and the assumptions that decide between them. For one-year mode discuss being right about the business but wrong about timing. Give valuation-based review/profit-taking conditions, measurable fundamental invalidation and deadline review, without fabricated price triggers. For long-term mode give operating milestones and permanent-loss indicators to revisit after filings. No automatic trades or reminders.
